Declared Sarvam parameter: Voice Experience

A faithful translator can still manufacture a dispute.

NyayBandhan is a live, cross-language call between two parties with a mediator sitting in the middle — one that tracks what was actually agreed, and refuses to draft a clause that isn't a real agreement.

See how it works View the code

Both said yes. Both meant different things.

A migrant tenant rents a flat from a landlord who speaks a different language. A broker relays every sentence perfectly. Six months later, "maintenance was separate" turns out to have meant two different things — and the deposit is gone.

Landlord
"Maintenance will be separate — whatever the actual cost is."
Tenant
"Okay, maintenance separate. ₹500 a month, fixed."
DIVERGED. Both parties said yes. A translation layer would relay both sentences correctly and let the deal close — with neither side realising they just agreed to two different things.

The system tracks a state, not just a translation

Every term in the negotiation — rent, deposit, maintenance — moves through a state machine. Only one state is safe to put in front of a lawyer.

AGREED

Both sides, same value. Safe to draft.

DIVERGED

Both said yes — to different values. The state no pure translator can produce.

HEDGED

A soft non-answer — "we'll see" — never counted as agreement.

PROPOSED

On the table, including open haggling that hasn't landed yet.

Speech in, mediation in the middle, speech out

Each turn is transcribed, folded into the term sheet, and either relayed or interjected on — then spoken back in the listener's language.

Party A mic ──WS──┐                            ┌── notes panel (term sheet + captions)
                  ├──→  FastAPI relay  ──────→ ┤
Party B mic ──WS──┘         │                  └── notes panel (term sheet + captions)
                            │
  per VAD segment:  Sarvam STT WS ──→ /translate ──→ live captions to the OTHER party
                            │
  on VAD end-of-turn:  sarvam-30b + tools ──→ mediator state ──→ both panels
                            │
                      Bulbul TTS ──→ one spoken relay sentence to the listener

Built on Sarvam's voice stack

One declared parameter — voice experience — carried end to end.

Speech in
Saaras v3

23 languages, code-mix native. Word timestamps and server-side VAD segment each turn.

Speech out
Bulbul v3

11 languages, 30+ voices. Each party gets a distinct voice; pace shifts for sensitive moments.

Translation
Mayura v1

Code-mixed for on-screen notes, formal for anything spoken aloud.

Reasoning
gpt-4o-mini

Tool-calling mediator agent, swappable to sarvam-30b with one env var.

Questions people actually ask

Isn't this just translation?
Translation is the easy half, and this gets it free. The hard half is that a correct translation still lets two people agree to different things. That's the state the term sheet tracks — and a translator can't.
What happens if the two sides just disagree?
The mediator escalates: it surfaces options, then names the exact gap, then parks the term and moves on. You still get an agreement covering everything that was genuinely settled.
Does it remember past negotiations?
Same two people, next negotiation: it opens knowing what they settled last time and makes them restate anything they want carried over, rather than assuming it.
